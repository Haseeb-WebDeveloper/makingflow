Every time someone completes one of your forms, MakingFlow POSTs the response to a URL you
choose, as JSON. This page is everything you need to build the endpoint that receives it.

Webhooks are configured **per form**, in MakingFlow under **the form → Integrations → Webhooks**.
A form can have several endpoints, and each one gets its own copy of every response.

## The short version

- We POST JSON. Reply **2xx** within <Val name="webhook.timeoutSeconds" /> seconds.
- If you set a secret, verify the signature over `timestamp.body` — not the body alone.
- Delivery is **at least once**. Deduplicate on `X-MakingFlow-Delivery-Id`.
- Failures retry for <Val name="webhook.retryWindow" />, then stop.

## What we send [#payload]

One POST per completed response, per endpoint. Only completed responses — a partially filled form
that is never submitted does not fire.

`value` takes the shape of the field that produced it: a string, a number, a boolean, an array of
strings for multi-select, or an object for structured fields like file uploads. Write your handler
to tolerate all of them.

`question` is the field's label at the time of submission, included so a delivery reads on its own.
It changes if the form is edited, so key your logic on `fieldId`, which does not.

<Callout variant="security">
Answers are written by whoever filled in your form. Treat every value as untrusted input: escape it
before rendering, never pass it to a shell or a query without parameterising.
</Callout>

## Verifying the signature [#verify]

Optional but strongly recommended. Without it, anyone who learns your endpoint URL can post fake
responses to it. Add a secret when you create the webhook, and we sign every delivery with it.

<Callout variant="warning">
The signature covers `` `${timestamp}.${body}` `` — the timestamp from the header, a full stop, then
the raw body. Signing the body alone is the single most common mistake, and it fails every time
with no clue why.
</Callout>

Two more things that will cost you an afternoon if missed:

- **Use the raw request body.** Parsing the JSON and re-serialising it reorders keys and changes
  whitespace, so the HMAC will not match even though the data is identical.
- **Check the timestamp.** Reject anything more than <Val name="webhook.replayWindowMinutes" />
  minutes old. Skip this and a captured delivery can be replayed at you forever, which is the entire
  reason the timestamp is in there.

## Retries, and why you may see a delivery twice [#retries]

Anything that is not a 2xx is retried. A 3xx counts as a failure — we do not follow redirects, so
point the webhook at its final URL.

<WebhookRetryTable />

Intervals are jittered by up to <Val name="webhook.jitterPercent" /> so that a receiver coming back
up is not hit by every queued delivery in the same instant. After attempt
<Val name="webhook.maxAttempts" /> we stop and mark the delivery failed; the form owner can still
resend it by hand.

<Callout>
Delivery is **at least once**, not exactly once. If you accept a delivery but your response is slow
enough for us to time out, we will retry one you already processed. Keep a record of handled
`X-MakingFlow-Delivery-Id` values and ignore repeats — the id is stable across retries, and across a
manual resend.
</Callout>

**Order is not guaranteed.** A delivery that needed three attempts arrives after one that succeeded
first time, even if it was submitted earlier. Sort by `submission.submittedAt` rather than arrival
order.

## Responding

Reply **2xx** as soon as you have the delivery safely stored, then do the real work. We wait
**<Val name="webhook.timeoutSeconds" /> seconds** and treat anything slower as a failure — so an
endpoint that finishes the job before replying gets retried, and processes everything twice.

Your response body is kept and shown to the form owner, truncated, so a short error message here is
the fastest way to tell them what went wrong.

## Testing your endpoint

**Send test** in the webhook settings posts a sample payload immediately and shows the status you
returned. The test body carries an extra `"test": true` field and is signed exactly like a real
delivery, so it exercises your verification code properly. It is not recorded as a delivery and is
never retried.

While developing, a request-inspector service such as webhook.site gives you a public URL you can
point at and watch. Note the next section before reaching for a tunnel to your laptop.

## Which URLs we can reach

The request comes from our servers, not from the browser of the person filling in your form. So the
URL has to be reachable from the public internet: a `localhost` address points at our machine, not
yours, and private and link-local ranges are refused outright.

For local development, use a tunnel that gives you a public hostname. And plan without a firewall
allowlist: we run on serverless infrastructure with no fixed egress addresses, so there is no stable
set of IPs to permit.

## What we don't do yet

Stated plainly so you can design around it rather than discover it:

- **One event.** `submission.created` is all we send. Publishing a form, editing one, or deleting a
  response fire nothing.
- **Per form, not per workspace.** A form created later starts with no webhooks — it does not
  inherit them.
- **No delivery API.** Delivery history is visible in the app; there is no endpoint to poll for it.
- Delivery records, including the body we sent, are kept for **<Val name="webhook.retentionDays" />
  days**. Deleting a response deletes its delivery records with it, so a resend is no longer
  possible.

## A note on the older signature

Deliveries currently carry a second header, `X-MakingFlow-Signature`, in the format `sha256=<hex>`,
signing the body alone. It exists only so integrations built before the timestamped format keep
working, and it will be removed.

Do not build against it. It cannot express a replay window, so a delivery captured once can be
replayed at your endpoint indefinitely. If you are verifying it today, move to
`X-MakingFlow-Signature-V2`, which is already being sent alongside.
